# EPISTLE VIII AUTHENTICITY 0001 — from older acceptance to probable inauthenticity

**Status:** Phase 1 provisional assessment  
**PROJECT PLATO grade:** **C — probably inauthentic**

## Why C rather than B or D

Letter VIII has a distinctive reception history.

Older modern scholarship often accepted it together with Letter VII. Victoria Wohl's survey records that VII and VIII enjoyed the strongest support among the thirteen letters.

More recent specialist work is markedly more skeptical. Carol Atack's 2020 *Classical Quarterly* article treats the Eighth Letter explicitly as **pseudo-Platonic**, interpreting it as a later response to Plato's literary method and political thought and examining the historical difficulties of its supposed Sicilian setting.

Malcolm Schofield likewise singles out the Eighth Letter as a case where historical problems may offer unusually strong grounds against authenticity.

## Project decision

The present evidence is stronger against authorship than in the genuinely divided case of Letter VII, so VIII is not B.

At the same time, its substantial history of scholarly acceptance distinguishes it from the clearest extra-canonical spuria. PROJECT PLATO therefore records:

**C — probably inauthentic.**

This is a CUSTODIAN-only authorship judgment and does not remove the letter from the historical corpus catalog.
