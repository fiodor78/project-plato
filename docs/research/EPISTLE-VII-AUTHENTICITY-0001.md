# EPISTLE VII AUTHENTICITY 0001 — genuinely disputed

**Status:** Phase 1 provisional assessment  
**PROJECT PLATO grade:** **B — disputed**

## Why B

Letter VII cannot responsibly be reduced to either “accepted” or “forgery” in the project metadata.

There are substantial, specialist arguments on both sides.

### Against authenticity

Myles Burnyeat and Michael Frede mounted a major modern case that the letter is pseudo-Platonic. Nicholas Denyer's review in *Oxford Studies in Ancient Philosophy* criticizes some details of their case but endorses and strengthens the conclusion against authenticity.

Reference:
https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780198795797.003.0008

### For authenticity

Noburu Notomi (2022) argues specifically against the recent Anglophone skeptical case. He places Plato's possible use of letters in the wider fourth-century literary competition with Isocrates and concludes that the traditional attribution of Letter VII should be respected.

Reference:
https://doi.org/10.14195/2183-4105_23_5

### Continuing modern use

Ariel Helfer's 2023 monograph treats the Platonic Letters as philosophically significant evidence while addressing provenance and authenticity questions rather than assuming them away.

Reference:
https://doi.org/10.7591/cornell/9781501772894.003.0001

## Project consequence

Letter VII is **B**, not A and not C/D.

This is exactly the type of case for which the authenticity layer was designed: the historical text remains in the catalog while CUSTODIAN records the unresolved authorship dispute explicitly.

The B grade is CUSTODIAN-only and must not be exposed to PLATO unless deliberately introduced as later ACQUIRED knowledge.
