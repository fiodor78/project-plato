# EXTRA-CANONICAL EPISTLES 0001 — letters outside the canonical thirteen

**Status:** Phase 1 source-verified scope expansion

## Discovery

The initial PROJECT PLATO catalog treated the thirteen Thrasyllan Epistles as the complete epistolary attribution layer.

Recent corpus-history scholarship makes clear that this is too narrow for the project's goal of preserving the **historical attribution corpus**.

Tobias Hirsch (2026), following Forcignanò and Martinelli Tempesta (2023), explicitly points to nine additional letters of “Plato” outside the canonical thirteen:

**Hercher 14, 15, 24, 25, 26, 30, 31, 70, 85.**

## Why they belong in the catalog

These texts do not belong to the Thrasyllan thirteen-letter collection, but they are historically transmitted under Plato's name in later epistolographic traditions.

For PROJECT PLATO this is sufficient for **historical catalog inclusion**.

It is not sufficient for admission to ORIGINAL runtime knowledge.

## Authenticity

Forcignanò and Martinelli Tempesta describe the extra letters as indisputably spurious. They therefore belong to the historical attribution layer, not to the authentic core.

Individual D assertions will be created only after the direct textual/editorial records are normalized sufficiently for each item.

## Current entry policy

The nine letters are registered as:

- `EXTANT`
- `EXTRA_CANONICAL_EXTANT_ATTRIBUTION`
- `PENDING_REVIEW` for runtime candidacy
- `CUSTODIAN_ONLY`

Where recipient/content information is secure from the modern corpus-history discussion, it is noted. Where it is not yet directly normalized from Hercher/Hermann, the catalog deliberately leaves the description generic rather than guessing.

## Consequence

The historical catalog expands from **86 to 95 entries**.

This discovery is an example of why PROJECT PLATO separates “the texts in a familiar modern complete edition” from the broader history of materials transmitted or attributed under Plato's name.
