# CORPUS SCOPE RESEARCH 0001 — historical layers of the Platonic corpus

**Status:** Phase 1 working research note

## Why a layered catalog is necessary

Ancient and modern sources do not supply one homogeneous category called “the works of Plato.”

The evidence includes:

1. a Thrasyllan canon arranged in nine tetralogies;
2. ancient lists of works explicitly regarded as spurious;
3. later/editorial collections of pseudo-Platonic material;
4. anthology material attributed to Plato;
5. titles that are attested but no longer extant.

The project therefore records source-specific attestations rather than converting all evidence immediately into one authenticity judgment.

## Primary ancient anchor: Diogenes Laertius III.57–62

Diogenes reports Thrasyllus' arrangement of Plato in nine tetralogies. The ninth ends with the Epistles, explicitly said to be thirteen in number.

Diogenes then separately states that certain dialogues are acknowledged as spurious, naming:

- Midon / Horse-breeder;
- Eryxias / Erasistratus;
- Alcyon;
- Acephali / Sisyphus;
- Axiochus;
- Phaeacians;
- Demodocus;
- Chelidon;
- Seventh Day;
- Epimenides.

## Modern scholarship on dubia/spuria

Katarzyna Jażdżewska, *Greek Dialogue in Antiquity: Post-Platonic Transformations* (Oxford, 2022), distinguishes dubious works inside the Thrasyllan canon from texts outside it commonly discussed as Appendix Platonica. The chapter treats, among others, Epinomis, Hipparchus, Minos, Theages, Cleitophon, Alcibiades II, Rival Lovers, Sisyphus, Eryxias, On Justice, On Virtue, Demodocus, Axiochus and Halcyon.

This source supports treating “Thrasyllan dubia” and “Appendix/spuria” as analytically distinct, while still avoiding a project-imposed single immutable definition of Appendix Platonica.

DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780192893352.003.0005

## Epigrams

Perseus catalogs `Epigrammata` under the Plato textgroup, with editions deriving from the Greek Anthology tradition.

Source:
https://catalog.perseus.tufts.edu/catalog/urn:cts:greekLit:tlg0059.tlg039

Modern scholarship warns that at least the love epigrams attributed to Plato are Alexandrian in tone/style; this is precisely why anthology attribution must be recorded independently from modern authenticity.

## Working scope rule

The **historical attribution catalog** should be inclusive.

The **ORIGINAL runtime corpus release** remains selective only in the technical sense that it can contain extant text: catalog-only lost titles cannot become model knowledge.

Modern authenticity weighting/classification is handled later and does not delete historical entries from the catalog.
