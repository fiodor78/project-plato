# AUTHENTICITY METHODOLOGY 0001 — Plato profile

**Status:** Active Phase 1 methodology

## Research question

For each extant catalog entry:

> What is the strongest defensible current assessment of Platonic authorship, and how certain is that assessment?

This is different from asking whether the text belongs to the historical Platonic corpus.

## Evidence hierarchy

### 1. External ancient evidence

Examples:

- explicit references by Aristotle or other early authors;
- ancient catalog/canonical inclusion;
- ancient explicit rejection;
- testimony about alternative authorship.

Ancient evidence is evidence about reception and attribution, not automatic proof of authorship.

### 2. Modern work-specific scholarship

Prefer:

- critical-edition introductions;
- Oxford/Cambridge/Brill scholarly treatments;
- peer-reviewed articles/monographs focused on the work;
- major reference works where they discuss authenticity directly.

### 3. Stylometry and linguistic evidence

Use as one evidential family, not an oracle.

Stylometric conclusions depend on corpus choice, features, chronology assumptions and method.

### 4. Philosophical/literary dependence

Arguments based on apparent dependence on other Platonic works, doctrinal mismatch, characterization or literary quality must be recorded carefully. “This does not sound like Plato” is not sufficient evidence by itself.

## Working A–D interpretation

### A — strongly accepted

Use where modern authorship is treated as ordinary/secure rather than a live problem.

A does not mean every speech expresses Plato's own belief.

### B — disputed

Use where current serious scholarship remains divided.

Examples of the kind of works that require B-level scrutiny include Hippias Major, Alcibiades I, Clitophon and the Seventh Letter; the final grade must still be work-specific.

### C — probably inauthentic

Use where substantial modern consensus rejects Platonic authorship but the case is historically or methodologically less absolute than the extra-canonical spuria.

Several Thrasyllan dubia are likely to fall here after source-by-source review.

### D — pseudo-Platonic

Use for material whose Platonic authorship is overwhelmingly rejected, especially extra-canonical spuria or texts explicitly rejected in ancient testimony.

## Sources already identified for the assessment stage

- Christopher Rowe / Routledge Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Plato — Authenticity and chronology”: identifies the difficult cases and emphasizes legitimate scholarly disagreement.
  https://www.rep.routledge.com/articles/biographical/plato-427-347-bc/v-1/sections/authenticity-and-chronology

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Plato’s Shorter Ethical Works”: gives a work-specific account of the unresolved Hippias Major authenticity debate.
  https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/plato-ethics-shorter/

- Katarzyna Jażdżewska, “Platonic Dubia and the Appendix Platonica”, Oxford University Press (2022): treats Thrasyllan dubia and extra-canonical pseudo-Platonic dialogues distinctly.
  https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780192893352.003.0005

- Victoria Wohl, “Plato Avant la Lettre: Authenticity in Plato's Epistles”: documents the wide historical range of positions on the thirteen letters and treats the collection's authenticity problem as unresolved rather than binary.
  https://doi.org/10.1017/S0048671X00001566

- Myles Burnyeat and Michael Frede, *The Pseudo-Platonic Seventh Letter* (Oxford, 2015), as a major modern argument against authenticity of Letter VII.

- Cambridge Companion discussions of stylometry/chronology, used as methodological evidence rather than a direct blanket verdict.

## Guardrails

- Do not derive a grade from Wikipedia or another tertiary summary, though such sources can help locate scholarship.
- Do not infer authenticity from inclusion in Burnet.
- Do not infer authenticity from Thrasyllan inclusion.
- Do not equate ancient “spurious” language with a modern theory of deliberate forgery.
- Do not classify the Epistles only as one block.
- Record disagreement explicitly.

## Output

Accepted assessments will be stored as CUSTODIAN-only authenticity assertions referring to stable catalog/work IDs and bibliography/source IDs.

A summary table may be generated for human review, but the structured assertions are authoritative.
