# EXTRA-CANONICAL EPISTLES AUTHENTICITY 0001

**Status:** Phase 1 provisional assessment

## Scope

This note covers the nine Platonic epistles transmitted outside the traditional thirteen-letter collection and cataloged in PROJECT PLATO by Hercher number:

14, 15, 24, 25, 26, 30, 31, 70 and 85.

## Result

| Hercher | Grade | Working assessment |
|---|---:|---|
| 14 | D | pseudo-Platonic |
| 15 | D | pseudo-Platonic |
| 24 | D | pseudo-Platonic |
| 25 | D | pseudo-Platonic |
| 26 | D | pseudo-Platonic |

| 70 | D | pseudo-Platonic |
| 85 | D | pseudo-Platonic |

## Evidence

The recent corpus-history study by Filippo Forcignanò and Stefano Martinelli Tempesta explicitly treats the extra-canonical Platonic epistles and describes the non-Thrasyllan material as spurious:
https://doi.org/10.30965/9783657793891_012

Tobias Hirsch (2026) independently enumerates this same nine-item group as letters of 'Plato' outside the canonical thirteen and points readers to the 2023 corpus-history study:
https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/classical-quarterly/article/abs/putting-the-platonic-letters-in-order-a-pen-pal-breakup-and-the-legacy-of-dion/DDC0840A75BD5640B0628BE6D648E42A

### Exception: Hercher 31

Anthony Francis Natoli's 2004 monograph argues that the Thirty-first Socratic Letter to Philip is likely to be genuinely Platonic:
https://www.steiner-verlag.de/The-Letter-of-Speusippus-to-Philip-II/9783515083966

Because this is a substantial published defense, PROJECT PLATO does not place Hercher 31 in D. The provisional grade is C: probably inauthentic, but not overwhelmingly rejected in the same way as the remaining extra-canonical epistles.

## Runtime consequence

All nine remain CUSTODIAN-only authenticity assessments.

Historical transmission and authenticity remain separate dimensions. None of these letters enters PLATO's ORIGINAL runtime corpus merely because it was once attributed to Plato.
